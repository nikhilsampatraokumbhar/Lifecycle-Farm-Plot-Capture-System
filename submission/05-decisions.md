# The fifteen decisions

### Lifecycle Farm Plot Capture · supporting note

**The submission stands on its own.** Nothing in it needs this page to make sense.

This is the working underneath it. Every choice here meant giving something up, and none of them is recorded without the thing it cost, because a choice with no downside is not a choice, it is a preference.

**Two ideas sit under all fifteen.** A plot is not a shape, it is a shape with a history, so every visit adds a record and nothing is ever overwritten. And the whole design has to fit inside **eleven minutes per farmer**, because anything that does not fit in the day gets faked, and faked data is worse than none.

---

#### What we are optimising for

**D1 · The number we are trying to move is the area gap: 15 to 25% down to under 5%.** The other three problems in the brief are symptoms of it. *Cost: settlement disputes would have been the louder pitch. Area variance is the more honest one.*

**D2 · When the company's data needs and the technician's time collide, his time wins.** He can opt out invisibly, and a worked-around app produces data that is confidently wrong. *Cost: richer notes, more photos and more confirmation taps do not make v1, and I have to defend that.*

#### The field method

**D3 · Walk the boundary properly once, at onboarding, then only ever mark what changed.** Walking every visit does not fit in eleven minutes, and what does not fit gets faked. *Cost: the first capture has to be right, because a whole season sits on it. Onboarding gets slower and stricter.*

**D4 · Accuracy standards vary by stage and by plot size, and a capture is never blocked.** An app that says no to a man alone in a field with no signal is an app that gets worked around. *Cost: some low-quality records enter the system. The alternative is not clean data, it is a technician who stops using it.*

**D5 · The farmer sees the number in his own language and signs, there and then.** Sixty seconds now prevents the dispute five months later with money on the table, and if he is watching, the capture cannot be faked. *Cost: sixty seconds per farmer is twenty minutes a day, and it works against D2 if I am not careful.*

#### Scope

**D6 · Build for all six stages, switch on three at launch.** Onboarding, sowing and post-harvest are where money changes hands. The rest drive forecasting. *Cost: the full-season story is what the brief describes and is more impressive. I have to defend a narrower launch as prioritisation.*

**D7 · One capture screen. The six visits differ by a table of rules, not by six pieces of software.** It decides whether the office can add a new visit type, or whether that needs developers and a month to reach two hundred phones. *Cost: more work upfront, and a screen serving six jobs is never perfect at any one of them.*

**D8 · Buy the map and the satellite imagery. Build the capture, the rules and the office side.** Nobody pays us for having drawn a map. And crops do not wait: buying puts us in a real field in three months, building puts us there in twelve. *Cost: dependent on someone else's pricing and uptime, and switching later is painful.*

#### Trust, conflict and authority

**D9 · Small area gaps pass, medium ones go to a supervisor, large ones freeze input release.** Automatic amendment is reckless and reviewing everything gets rubber-stamped, so bands put attention where the money is. *Cost: the 5% and 20% thresholds are mine, not the field's. They need a season of real captures.*

**D10 · Two technicians capture the same farm: keep both, and a supervisor decides.** You cannot merge two shapes, and "whoever synced last wins" is a coin toss with extra steps. *Cost: a queue someone has to work through. If it is not small, the real problem is how work is being allocated.*

**D11 · Make honest capture the fastest path, then add checks that cost the technician nothing.** If the honest path does not fit in the day, we designed the shortcut ourselves. *Cost: none of it is airtight. I am making faking harder than doing it properly, not impossible.*

#### The practical decisions

**D12 · Download today's club and tomorrow's, the night before rather than in the morning.** At 6:40 in the morning a progress bar gets skipped. At 9pm it does not, and everything else rests on this happening. *Cost: if he is redirected to a different village, the buffer does not cover it.*

**D13 · A Club is a real record with its own history, not a label on a farmer.** The work is club-shaped, and an app built around scattered farm visits fights how the job actually works. *Cost: more complexity, and messy cases to handle: plots in two villages, farmers who change club mid-season.*

**D14 · Two languages. Pictures before words. Spoken notes, never typed. No settings screen.** A small training budget is not a constraint to work around, it is the design brief. *Cost: icon-led design is ambiguous the first time, and I only find out which parts by watching people use it.*

**D15 · Pay a base on volume, but make the variable part depend on how the record was made.** You cannot design around a pay slip. And if the bonus depends on captures coming back clean, he will simply stop reporting problems. *Cost: harder to administer, and the payback shows up in the input budget rather than in his line.*

---

#### And six things I do not know

Every accuracy threshold here rests on numbers I assumed rather than measured. The 5% and 20% flag bands are mine, not the field's. I do not know how often the farmer is actually present, how large the map files really are, what happens today when contracted and actual area disagree, or what a technician's day genuinely looks like. My model comes from the brief's numbers, not from walking one. **I would spend two days with a technician before locking any of it.**
