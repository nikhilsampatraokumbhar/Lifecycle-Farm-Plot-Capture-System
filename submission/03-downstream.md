# What the data is worth after the visit

**A technician spends three minutes on a plot, for his own reason: recording what was sown.** Everything on this page is a second or seventh use of that same record. Nobody makes another visit or spends another minute in a field. That is the argument for calling this a platform rather than a feature: **the marginal cost of the next use is zero.**

### Input allocation

Today seed and fertiliser go out against **contracted** area. When a farmer sows 1.8 of his 2.4 acres, a quarter of those inputs are surplus, and surplus inputs do not sit in a shed.

Allocating against **sown** area needs only two of the six stages and shows up in the input budget in the first season. This is the use I would put in the business case, because it returns money inside one crop cycle.

### Yield estimation

Today the forecast is contracted area times expected yield, wrong from day one by the same 15 to 25% the brief opens with. With multi-stage capture it becomes area sown, adjusted at germination, reduced by pest damage, confirmed against standing area before harvest.

**The value is not a better number, it is a number that improves four times a season on a known schedule.**

### Insurance

Indemnity cover pays against proven loss, and the pest-stage capture *is* that proof: a dated, photographed, signed boundary of the affected area, which is what an assessor otherwise spends a day producing. Parametric cover needs accurate insured area, which is our onboarding number.

And the commercial point: **insurers price uncertainty.** Handing them 5% area accuracy instead of 20% is a negotiating position on premium.

### Farmer scoring

Whether a farmer sows what he contracts, season after season, is a behaviour signal that is **observed rather than self-reported**, which is what credit decisions lack.

**The caution:** a low score can mean a failed borewell, not a dishonest farmer. The reason captured in the field has to travel with the score, or we penalise people for drought.

### Procurement planning

Standing area at pre-harvest, rolled up by club and village, says how much is coming and roughly when. Today that is a guess extrapolated from contracts signed five months earlier.

Harvest logistics is a **timing and location** problem as much as a volume one, and the pre-harvest capture gives all three, two to three weeks ahead.

### Traceability

Export buyers and certification schemes increasingly ask which plot a consignment came from, and a dated boundary with its stage history answers that.

The sharper case is the class of regulation requiring proof that a plot was not forest before a cutoff date. A stored boundary plus historic satellite imagery answers exactly that, and nothing without a boundary can.

### Club analytics

Because a club is a real record, comparison costs nothing. One club at 95% of contracted area sown and its neighbour at 78% is worth a visit, and the answer is usually a borewell, a seed batch or a person.

**It also points inward.** If one technician's clubs consistently read differently from everyone else's, that is a training or data quality problem, and there is no other way we would see it.

**None of this needs a new visit, a new device, or one more minute in a field.**
