# What the data is worth after the visit

**A technician spends about three minutes on a plot, for his own reason: recording what was actually sown.** Everything on this page is a second, third or seventh use of that same record. Nobody makes another visit or spends another minute in a field.

That is the argument for calling this a platform rather than a feature: **the marginal cost of the next use of this data is zero.**

### Input allocation · the one that pays for the system

Today seed and fertiliser go out against **contracted** area. When a farmer sows 1.8 of his 2.4 acres, a quarter of those inputs are surplus, and surplus inputs do not sit in a shed.

Allocating against **sown** area needs only two of the six stages and shows up directly in the input budget in the first season. This is the use I would put in the business case, because it returns money inside one crop cycle rather than in a strategy deck.

### Yield estimation · a forecast that tightens on a schedule

Today the forecast is contracted area times expected yield, wrong from day one by the same 15 to 25% the brief opens with. With multi-stage capture it becomes area actually sown, adjusted at germination, reduced by pest-affected area, confirmed against standing area before harvest.

**The value is not a better number, it is a number that improves four times a season on a known schedule.** A forecast that tightens predictably is worth more to procurement than one sharper guess in December.

### Insurance · the loss report already exists

Indemnity cover pays against proven loss, and the pest-stage capture *is* that proof: a dated, photographed, signed boundary of exactly the affected area, which is what an assessor otherwise spends a day producing. Parametric cover needs no loss proof but does need accurate insured area, which is our onboarding number.

And the commercial point: **insurers price uncertainty.** Handing them 5% area accuracy instead of 20% is a negotiating position on premium, not just a nicer dataset.

### Farmer scoring · with one caution I would insist on

Whether a farmer sows what he contracts, season after season, is a behaviour signal that is **observed rather than self-reported**, which is exactly what credit decisions lack.

**The caution:** a low compliance score can mean a failed borewell, not a dishonest farmer. So the reason captured in the field has to travel with the score, all the way to whoever prices his credit. Without that we would be quietly penalising farmers for drought, and that is both wrong and the kind of thing that ends a contract farming relationship.

### Procurement planning · volume, timing and place

Standing area at pre-harvest, rolled up by club and village, says how much is coming and roughly when. Today that is a guess extrapolated from contracts signed five months earlier.

Harvest logistics is not only a volume problem, it is a **timing and location** problem, and the pre-harvest capture gives all three two to three weeks ahead. That is enough lead time to book transport and warehouse space.

### Traceability · answering a question we will be asked

Export buyers and certification schemes increasingly ask which plot a consignment came from, and a dated boundary with its stage history answers that. The sharper case is the class of regulation requiring proof that a plot was not forest before a cutoff date. A stored boundary plus historic satellite imagery answers exactly that, and nothing without a boundary can. We would be building the evidence years before anyone asks for it, which is the only time it is cheap to build.

### Club analytics · and the part that measures us

Because a club is a real record, comparison costs nothing. One club at 95% of contracted area sown and its neighbour at 78% is a question worth a visit, and the answer is usually a borewell, a seed batch or a person.

**It also points inward.** If one technician's clubs consistently read differently from everyone else's, that is a training or a data quality problem, and there is no other way we would see it. A system that only ever scores farmers is missing half of what it can see.

**None of this needs a new visit, a new device, or one more minute in a field.** It only needs the capture to have been recorded properly the first time, which is what the whole design has been about.
