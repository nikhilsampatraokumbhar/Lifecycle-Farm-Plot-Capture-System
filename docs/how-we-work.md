# Ground rules for this project

Short file. These are the rules I set for how this submission gets built.

---

## 1. No jargon. Anywhere.

Not in the submission, not in my notes, not in conversation.

If I can't say a sentence out loud in an interview without sounding like I copied it from somewhere, it doesn't go in. Every idea has to survive being said in plain words.

Rule of thumb: **if my mother wouldn't follow the sentence, rewrite it.**

There is a list at the end of the decision log of the words I'm deliberately avoiding, and what I say instead.

## 2. The screens should be genuinely good.

The brief says "fidelity matters less than clarity" and "we're not looking for pixel-perfect designs."

I'm doing good screens anyway.

Reason: the brief is telling me I *won't be penalised* for rough work. It is not telling me I'll be penalised for good work. This is a senior role. Screens that look like a real product do two things a napkin sketch can't — they prove I can hold a whole product in my head, and they give me something to point at.

So: clean, realistic mobile screens. Proper spacing, real labels, real numbers in them. Not scrappy boxes, and not fake-polished either — the point is that someone could hand these to a designer and an engineer and they'd both know what to do.

## 3. Show the thinking, not just the answer.

The submission is the answer. This decision log is the thinking.

Two separate documents. The submission goes in the 15 pages. The decision log goes alongside it, because the choices — and the things I chose *not* to do — are the actual proof that I can run a product, not just describe one.

## 4. Every choice records what it cost.

No decision goes in the log without a line saying what I gave up to make it. A choice with no downside isn't a choice, it's a preference.

## 5. Page budget is real.

Max 15 pages including screens. If screens go one-per-page they eat the whole document. So screens go 3-4 to a page. The budget is in the decision log and I hold to it.
